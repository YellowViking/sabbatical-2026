# Europe backpacking essentials - buy list

**Trip fit:** Aug 24 through roughly mid-November; Scandinavia and the Baltics first, then Slovenia, Croatia, and Italy. Hostel-heavy, rail/bus/ferry transfers, city walking, and non-technical day hikes. See the [canonical itinerary](../itinerary.md).

**Last triaged:** Aug 18, 2026 - five days before departure.

The goal is not to buy a travel-store catalog. Lay out what you already own, test it, and buy only the gaps below.

## Pack target

| Constraint | Target |
|---|---|
| **Main bag** | **35-40 L**, comfortable for station-to-hostel walks; confirm each intra-Europe fare's dimensions and weight |
| **Day bag** | **15-20 L**, light enough for city days but stable with water and rain layers |
| **Weight** | Aim for **8-10 kg in the main bag before food and water**; lighter is better on stairs and transfers |
| **Shoes** | **One worn-in walking/trail pair + shower sandals**; add a second closed shoe only for a real use case |
| **Laundry cycle** | Pack for **5-7 days**, then wash; trip length does not require more clothing |
| **Weather system** | Quick-dry base layer + fleece + light insulation + waterproof shell; combine instead of carrying one heavy coat |

The ticketed SAS entry flight includes a checked bag, but later low-cost fares may not. Do not size the whole trip around the first flight's allowance.

## Priority A - solve before departure

| Item | What to look for | Qty | Rough USD |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Waterproof shell** | Hood, fully taped seams, fits over fleece and light jacket; not merely "water-resistant" | 1 | $80-220 |
| **Grippy walking/trail shoes** | Comfortable for 15-20k-step city days and wet stone; use an existing worn-in pair if possible | 1 pair | $80-160 |
| **Fleece or active midlayer** | Quick-dry, not bulky cotton | 1 | $35-100 |
| **Light insulated jacket** | Packable synthetic or down layer for Nordic wind, ferries, and cool evenings | 1 | $60-180 |
| **Travel pack** | 35-40 L, good hip/shoulder fit, lockable zips helpful; test loaded before keeping | 1 | $100-250 |
| **Waterproof pack liner** | Purpose-built liner or a thick compactor bag; protects contents when a cover leaks | 1 | $5-25 |
| **Type C/E/F/L adapter** | Grounded universal adapter covering continental Europe and Italy; no voltage conversion needed for dual-voltage chargers | 1 | $20-40 |
| **USB-C charger** | Dual-voltage 100-240 V, about 65 W, 2-3 ports; charge phone and other devices from one outlet | 1 | $35-75 |
| **Power bank** | 10,000-20,000 mAh from a reputable maker, clearly labeled and **under 100 Wh** | 1 | $25-60 |
| **Hostel padlocks** | Small combination locks; make one a thin-shackle model for narrow locker holes | 2 | $12-25 |
| **Prescription supply** | Full fall trip plus a small delay buffer, in labeled original containers; verify rules for controlled medicines | as needed | varies |

**Five-day footwear rule:** do not replace a comfortable pair with stiff new boots just because the trip includes hikes. If footwear truly must be replaced, buy locally, wear it continuously before departure, and keep the return option.

### Electronics

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| USB-C cables | 2 | One normal and one longer cable; make sure both support the charging power needed |
| Wired earbuds | 1 | Reliable flight/ferry backup even if carrying wireless earbuds |
| Short extension lead | optional | Only a compact, **230 V-rated** lead without surge electronics; never use a US-only power strip |
| Luggage tracker | optional | Helpful for a checked bag; also use a normal name/phone tag |

Phones and laptops normally accept 100-240 V, but read each charger's input label. A plug adapter changes the plug shape; it does not make a 120 V-only appliance safe. Leave single-voltage hair tools at home.

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| **Blister kit** | 1 small kit | Hydrocolloid dressings, tape, and a few regular bandages |
| **Personal medicines** | small supply | Pain reliever, antihistamine, anti-diarrheal, and oral rehydration salts only if normally safe for you |
| Antiseptic wipes + tweezers | a few / 1 | Keep the kit compact |
| **Tick remover** | 1 | Useful for Scandinavia, Baltics, Slovenia, and Croatia nature days |
| **Picaridin insect repellent** | 1 small bottle | Late-summer mosquitoes and ticks; follow the label |
| **SPF 30+ sunscreen** | 1 small bottle | Replace locally when empty |
| Toiletries | 1 week | Carry-on sizes, generally no container over 100 mL |
| Hand sanitizer | 1 small bottle | Refill or replace locally |

Keep medicines in the cabin bag, not checked luggage. Medical choices are personal: use products you tolerate and ask a clinician or pharmacist about contraindications.

### Documents and payment

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Small document pouch | 1 | Passport, backup card, insurance details, and paper border copies |
| Backup payment card | 1 | Store separately from the primary wallet |
| Printed emergency sheet | 2 copies | Contacts, insurance number, card-cancel numbers, and passport copy; redact unnecessary financial data |

Do not put every card, all cash, and the passport in the same removable pouch.

## Non-physical purchases to finish

- **Travel medical insurance:** medical plus evacuation/repatriation; the itinerary's target is $250k-$1M medical coverage.
- **Norway-inclusive eSIM:** install before leaving if convenient, but activate according to the plan's instructions so the validity clock does not start early.
- **Cloud/offline backup:** save passport, insurance, bookings, and emergency contacts in an encrypted account available without the physical phone; also download offline maps.

## Do not panic-buy

- **55-70 L trekking pack:** too much capacity for this hostel/transit trip.
- **Sleeping bag or sleep liner:** normal hostels provide bedding, and some prohibit outside sleeping bags; buy only for a specific hut requirement.
- **Heavy hiking boots:** unnecessary for the planned non-technical hikes unless already preferred and broken in.
- **More than two pairs of shoes:** weight and bulk rise quickly.
- **Heavy cotton hoodie, bulky towel, or full-size toiletries:** slow to dry and easy to replace locally.
- **Voltage converter:** unnecessary for dual-voltage electronics; unsafe as a generic fix for high-wattage appliances.
- **Large US power strip or surge protector:** may not be rated for 230 V.
- **Knife, multitool, or large scissors:** cabin-security trouble for little value.
- **Trekking poles before flying:** awkward cabin restrictions; rent or buy locally only if a specific hike needs them.
- **Full camping/cooking kit:** not part of this hostel-based route.
- **Large money belt or anti-theft gimmick:** separate cards and attentive carry matter more.

## Flight and final-fit checks

1. Put spare lithium batteries and the power bank in the **cabin bag**, never checked luggage. Keep the power bank under 100 Wh and verify the operating airline's current rule.
2. Pack carry-on liquids to the conservative standard: containers no larger than 100 mL in one clear bag, even if an airport has newer scanners.
3. Measure and weigh both bags against **each booked fare**, not just the entry ticket.
4. Load everything, add 1 L of water, and walk for **30-45 minutes with stairs**. Fix hot spots or remove weight.
5. Remove anything that has no likely use in the first two weeks and cannot be bought cheaply in Europe.

## Budget reality

- **Already own pack, shoes, and layers:** roughly **$180-400** can cover the weather, power, hostel, and health gaps.
- **Starting without core gear:** roughly **$600-1,100**, depending mainly on pack, shell, shoes, and insulation.
- Insurance, eSIM, prescriptions, and replacement electronics are outside those ranges.

Buying less is part of the system: the best loadout is the lightest one that keeps the traveler warm, dry, charged, and able to sleep.
