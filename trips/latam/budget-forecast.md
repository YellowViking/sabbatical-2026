# Brazil leg: budget forecast (3 Dec 2026 – ~2 Jan 2027)

Built 24 Nov 2026 in the same format as the Europe forecast (`trips/europe-2026/budget-forecast.md`). It applies the Europe "lessons for the next budget" (`budget-actual.md`):
- **Beds are budgeted at dorm prices**, with private rooms as a separate variant line.
- **Intercity transport is priced per leg**, with a separate reserve for route changes.
- **The award flight is valued explicitly.**

All prices are in **USD**. Exchange rates used:
- **R$5.90 = $1.** Live mid-market rates on 23–24 Nov 2026 were 5.998 (ECB via Frankfurter, 23 Nov), 5.958 (open.er-api, 24 Nov 00:02 UTC) and 5.862 (AwesomeAPI bid, 24 Nov 10:19 BRT).
- **€1 = $1.094** (ECB, 23 Nov), used for Booking.com, which the signed-in account shows in EUR.

The route is Plan B: 29 nights in Brazil plus two travel days (3 Dec in the air, 2 Jan flying south).

## (a) Bottom line

**Carry about $4.2k: the ~$3.5k expected-spend ceiling plus a ~$0.7k lodging buffer for switching to private rooms. Expected spend at the dorm picks is about $2.9–3.5k for the leg, or $94–112 a day over 31 days.** That includes the ~$400 exit flight Salvador → south. Without that flight, the Brazil leg costs **about $2.5–3.1k**.

- **Living: about $1.48k, or ~$48/day.**
  - Dorm beds ~$0.52k (~$18/night)
  - Food ~$0.57k (~$18/day)
  - Three drinks a day ~$0.24k (~$2.70 a drink)
  - Local and routine costs ~$0.15k, including Jeri's R$41.50 visitor tax
- **On top of living:**
  - 10% cushion: ~$0.15k
  - All transport: ~$0.87–1.16k. That is the GRU→JJD flight, the exit flight, the buses, boats and transfers, and the $26 award taxes.
  - Route-change reserve: ~$0.1–0.15k
  - Sights and guides: ~$0.16–0.34k
  - E-visa, insurance and eSIM: ~$0.16–0.21k
- **Lodging buffer (private-room fallback), +~$0.7k:** every stop in the [itinerary](itinerary.md) names a **location-first private alternative**: Booking location ≥ 9.0, 30+ reviews, ≤10% bad, totals from the room tables (24 Nov). Taking all of them comes to **~$1,200 against ~$520 at the dorm picks**. It's carried as a buffer, not planned spend.
- **Private-room variant:** +$0.66k in lodging (+$0.73k with the cushion), so **about $3.6–4.2k**.
- **The award flight SEA→GIG (30,000 Alaska points + $26) is worth about $540.** On Google Flights the cheapest cash one-way for 3 Dec is **$565** (Air Canada via Toronto, 23 h 20 min); Air Canada via Vancouver and Toronto is $645, and Delta via JFK and Atlanta is $885. That values the points at **~1.8¢ each**, above the 1.3–1.5¢ bar. Counting it, **the leg's all-in value is about $3.45–4.0k.**

## (e) Levers

**Cheaper:**
- **Take the north-east privates, skip the Rio/Ilha Grande/Salvador ones.** Privates in Jeri, Canoa, Pipa, Olinda and Maceió cost only ~$90 more in total than dorms there.
- **Use buses rather than door-to-door transfers on the four north-east hops.** This saves ~$60–80, at the cost of longer days (Jeri → Canoa via Fortaleza is ~8–9 h).
- **Hike to Christ via Parque Lage and skip the Sugarloaf cable car** (Urca trail and Dois Irmãos instead). This saves $33–57.
- **Self-guide the Chapada days where it's legal and safe** (Ribeirão do Meio, Serrano, Primavera; Pai Inácio by bus), and take a guide only for Fumaça or Poço Encantado. This saves ~$50–100.
- **Cook at Christmas and New Year.** Many restaurants close on 24–25 Dec and 31 Dec–1 Jan, and most of the listed Salvador hostels have kitchens. It also avoids holiday menus.
- **Budget the exit flight in the next leg**, or pay for it with points if it's the New Year peak ($370–430). This takes ~$0.4k off this leg.
- **If Uruguay doesn't need it, fly out on 3–4 Jan instead of 2 Jan**, when the peak eases (unverified).

**Pricier:**
- **Private rooms everywhere:** +$0.66k.
- **Vale do Pati trek:** +$200–340.
- **Sugarloaf + Christ by van + boat tours in Ilha Grande and Paraty:** +$80–100.
- **Door-to-door transfers on every coastal leg:** +$100–150.
- **New Year booked late.** Today only 7 Salvador hostels sell beds for 31 Dec–2 Jan. If the cheap ones sell out, $45–70/night is plausible, so allow +$50–80.
- **Bus fares on holiday dates** run at 1.5–2× the "from" price, which is already partly in the band.

## (f) Against the backpacker consensus

- **The Broke Backpacker** ([Brazil guide](https://www.thebrokebackpacker.com/backpacking-brazil-budget-travel-guide/), updated 20 Nov 2026; read in the browser on 24 Nov):
  - Budget table "Total per day": **$26–52** for a backpacker, **$61–120** mid-range, **$155+** comfortable.
  - Rows (backpacker tier): accommodation $5–15, food $6–12, transport $5, nightlife $5–10, activities $5–10. Dorms run "$10–15 in all of the major cities, even in Rio".
  - "Relative comfort": **$40–50/day**.
- **Hostelworld** ([backpacking Brazil](https://www.hostelworld.com/blog/backpacking-brazil/), updated 21 Oct 2026; read on 24 Nov):
  - "Budget about **R$250 a day including travel**" (≈ **$42** at R$5.90).
  - Buses run about R$10 per hour on the road; hostels from R$20 a night.
  - Its sight prices are dated: it gives the Corcovado train as R$65–79, while the operator's own site shows R$139 for December.
- **This forecast's living cost is ~$48/day (dorm lean).** That's at the top of the Broke Backpacker's $26–52 backpacker range, and about 15% above Hostelworld's ~$42, which even includes travel. The gap has four causes:
  - **Three drinks a day: ~$8/day.** Without them it's ~$40/day, inside both ranges.
  - **December high season and the two Salvador holidays.**
  - **Jeri, Pipa and Ilha Grande:** touristy beach villages with food prices close to Rio's.
  - **No cooking assumed** except at the holidays.
- **Adding sights and ground transport inside Brazil** (no flights) gives ~$61–73/day. That is still well under Europe's actual $127/day cash.
- **Beds: Brazil's well-rated dorms cost ~$18/night, against Europe's actual $42/night** for mostly private rooms.
