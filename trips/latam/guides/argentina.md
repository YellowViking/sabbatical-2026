# Argentina: region notes

Part of the [LatAm itinerary](../itinerary.md). Stops: [Córdoba + Los Gigantes](cordoba.md) · [Cafayate](cafayate.md) · [Salta](salta.md) · [Quebrada de Humahuaca (Tilcara + Purmamarca)](quebrada-de-humahuaca.md)

**3. Argentina: Córdoba + Sierras → Cafayate → Salta → Quebrada de Humahuaca (stops 12–15, Sat 2 → Thu 14 Jan)**

**Exchange rate used:** USD at **ARS 1,650 = US$1**, between the official rate (1,615) and MEP (1,695) on 24 Nov (dolarapi.com). A foreign Visa/Mastercard is charged near the MEP rate, so card prices land a little under these USD figures. Plataforma10 shows its own USD at ≈1,592.

**Prices:** lodging totals are live checkout prices for 1 adult on the exact nights. Booking room tables showed no extra tax line; Hostelworld totals exclude its small booking fee.

**Bus timetables:** local operators (Aconquija, Evelia, Sarmiento) only sell about 1–3 weeks ahead on Plataforma10; long-distance lines and Andesmar's shop are already selling January. Where a January date isn't on sale yet, the times below come from the same weekday in December and are marked *(Dec timetable)*.

**Money.** Cards work in Córdoba and Salta. Carry pesos in cash for the things that matter on this stretch: COATA/Sarmiento tickets bought at the roadside, the Los Gigantes land fee, the Río Colorado guides, the Garganta del Diablo and Hornocal community fees, and the Hornocal 4x4. Foreign-card ATM withdrawals in Argentina carry a high fixed fee, so take fewer, larger withdrawals in Córdoba and Salta *(≈, from general reports)*. Small towns (Purmamarca, Tilcara) have one or two ATMs that can run dry at weekends.

**Weather.** Early January is the rainy season in the hills, with storms building after midday. Mornings are the dry window, so every hike below starts early. The ERA5 model grid gives Córdoba city rain on 41% of 1–20 January days (max ≈ 30 °C). The Sierras plateau (La Pampilla, Los Gigantes) gets rain on 64–69% of days at max ≈ 20 °C, with fog that can close RP34. The Quebrada gets rain on 63–71% *(the model grid overstates valley floors; Cafayate and Purmamarca sit in rain shadows)*.
