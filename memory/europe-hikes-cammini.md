## Cammini and long walks

### 1. Via Francigena — the fall-route winner

The Via Francigena runs from Canterbury to Rome. Its Tuscany stages sit directly between the planned Florence block and Rome, so this is the only genuine pilgrimage walk that belongs naturally in the fall route.

#### Default candidate — one walking day

**Monteriggioni → Siena · ~20.6 km · roughly 4.5–6h walking**

1. Move one Tuscany bed from Florence to **Monteriggioni**.
2. Walk to Siena with a small pack the next morning.
3. Sleep Siena and continue to Rome, or take the evening train onward if the walk finishes early.

This replaces the planned Siena day trip and can stay inside the existing **Florence + Tuscany 3–4n** block. It changes the bed pattern, not the total trip nights.

#### Bigger version — two walking days

| Day | Stage | Approx. distance | Reality check |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **San Gimignano → Monteriggioni** | ~30.5 km | Long day; start at dawn, carry water, and do it only in dry weather. |
| 2 | **Monteriggioni → Siena** | ~20.6 km | Friendlier finish through the Tuscan hills into Siena. |

This version is about **51 km** and needs San Gimignano/Monteriggioni logistics plus a luggage decision. Within a 4-night Tuscany block it cuts Florence very thin; adding a fifth block night requires an explicit trade from Gotland, Riga or elsewhere.

#### Pilgrim mechanics

- Use the official **Via Francigena app**, GPX and active-alert page; route markings do not replace offline navigation.
- A credential and stamps add pilgrim texture, but one or two stages are a **mini-cammino**, not a certificate chase.
- Do not assume luggage transfer. Carry the trip pack, leave the large bag in Florence and backtrack, or confirm a forward-transfer service before booking non-refundable beds.
- Early November means short daylight, possible mud and fewer seasonal services. The 30.5 km stage is a weather-dependent option, not a vow.

**Official:** [Via Francigena route and app](https://www.viefrancigene.org/en/) · [active alerts](https://www.viefrancigene.org/en/news/all-active-alerts-along-the-via-francigena)

### 2. Juliana Trail — scenic traverse, not a Camino

The official Juliana Trail is a **270 km circular route in 16 stages**, averaging about **17.5 km / 4–5h** per stage. It circles the Julian Alps below the highest peaks rather than summiting Triglav.

For this itinerary, use only a contiguous **Bled → Pokljuka/Goreljek → Bohinj** pair. It provides the through-walk feeling while staying lower than the high huts. Verify the intermediate hotel, baggage plan and October transport before replacing the current Vintgar/Vogel/Mostnica sampler.

**Official:** [Slovenian Tourist Board overview](https://www.slovenia.info/en/stories/juliana-trail-a-long-distance-hiking-trail)

### 3. Forest Trail / E11 — the Riga sponge walk

The Baltic Forest Trail is a **2,141 km** E11 route through Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia, divided into roughly **20 km one- or two-day sections** with accommodation and transport options. Its Gauja National Park segment is the useful part here.

Treat **Sigulda–Līgatne–Cēsis** as a planning corridor, not a self-invented line: select contiguous official stages after checking GPX, current alerts, beds and return transit. Two walking days can replace two Riga base days without increasing the Schengen total.

**Official:** [Baltic Forest Trail](https://baltictrails.eu/en/forest) · [itinerary / GPX](https://baltictrails.eu/en/forest/itinerary)

### 4. Full Camino de Santiago — save for spring Iberia

| Route | Walking shape | Why / why not |
|---|---|---|
| **Portuguese Way, Tui → Santiago** | **118.8 km · ~6 days** | Best first Camino for the provisional Portugal/Spain spring: medium-low difficulty, enough distance for the Compostela, and geographically coherent. |
| **Portuguese from Porto** | Roughly 10–12 days, depending inland/coastal choices | Better journey than only the minimum; pair coast and Galicia when spring dates are real. |
| **Camino Primitivo, Oviedo → Santiago** | Roughly 12–15 days · hillier/wetter | Best fit for hiking ambition and solitude; choose over the French Way when terrain matters more than social infrastructure. |
| **French Way, Sarria → Santiago** | Roughly 5–6 days / last ~115 km | Easiest social first Camino and simplest logistics, but the busiest and least distinctive choice for this nature-first trip. |
| **Santiago → Finisterre/Muxía** | ~3–5 additional days | Excellent Atlantic epilogue after reaching Santiago; it is an extension, not the qualifying approach to the Cathedral. |

Current Pilgrim Office rule: for a Compostela on foot, complete at least **100 continuous km on the same recognized route**, including the final stage into Santiago. If doing only the minimum segment, get dated credential stamps at the start and end of each stage.

**Official:** [Camino route portal](https://www.caminodesantiago.gal/en/make-plans/the-ways) · [Compostela requirements](https://oficinadelperegrino.com/en/pilgrimage/the-compostela/)

## Weather and safety gates

| Terrain | Check | Cancel / shorten when |
|---|---|---|
| **Norway mountain/fjord** | Yr forecast · Varsom · Entur/local bus · trail report | Strong wind, heavy rain, fog, ice, missed last bus, or a wet exposed descent. |
| **Baltic forest/bog** | Official stage alert · daylight · return transit | Flooded boardwalk/trail, hunting/closure notice, or no reliable return. |
| **Julian Alps** | Official trail status · first snow · cable/bus/lodging operation | Snow/ice, thunderstorms, closed intermediate bed, or high route requiring winter gear. |
| **Plitvice** | Open programmes, boats/trains and daylight | Long circuit is not offered or would force a rushed finish. |
| **Cinque Terre** | [Live park trail network](https://www.parconazionale5terre.it/Esentieri-outdoor.php) | Any closure, rain warning, wet stone, thunder or insufficient daylight. Manarola–Corniglia is currently shown closed; re-check near the trip. |
| **Tuscany / Amalfi** | Rain history · heat · daylight · bus exits | Heavy rain, muddy clay/stone, thunder, or a late start that pushes the finish into darkness. |

**Carry:** grippy trail shoes, shell, warm layer, headlamp, 1–2 L water capacity, small first-aid/blister kit, power bank and offline GPX. Poles earn their space for Aurlandsdalen, Tuscany mud and long stone descents.
