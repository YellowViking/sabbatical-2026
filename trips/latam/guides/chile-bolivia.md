# Chile + Bolivia: region notes

Part of the [LatAm itinerary](../itinerary.md). Stops: [San Pedro de Atacama](san-pedro-de-atacama.md) · [Jeep tour San Pedro → Uyuni](uyuni-jeep-tour.md) · [Potosí](potosi.md) · [Sucre](sucre.md) · [La Paz](la-paz.md) · [Copacabana + Isla del Sol](copacabana-isla-del-sol.md)

**4. Chile + Bolivia: San Pedro de Atacama → Uyuni → Potosí → Sucre → La Paz → Titicaca (14 → 29 Jan, 15 nights)**

**Money for this section (checked 24 Nov 2026).**
- **Chile (San Pedro):** CLP 1,024 = US$1 (open.er-api, 24 Nov). Card works almost everywhere in San Pedro; carry some CLP cash for park gates and the minibus tips. Tour agencies quote CLP and USD: paying the CLP price is cheaper (Cordillera Traveller CLP 245,000 ≈ $239 vs its own $260 USD price).
- **Bolivia:** the boliviano **floated on 26 June 2026**. ✅ Banco Central de Bolivia official rate on Tue 24 Nov 2026: **Bs 11.78 = US$1** (bcb.gob.bo home page; it moved between ≈ Bs 9.7 and 12.3 since June). Old 6.96 prices and the old "blue dollar" trick are gone: AI-summary leads (La Paz Life, Bus Hop, salardeuyuni.com, Sep–Nov 2026) say the parallel rate is now within ~1–2% of the official one and foreign cards/ATMs convert at the market rate. ATMs cap withdrawals at ≈ Bs 2,000–2,800 per go, some banks charge Bs 0–50, and **ATMs in Uyuni and Copacabana run dry at weekends; there are none on Isla del Sol or on the jeep tour.** Small shops add 5–10% for cards and local QR payments don't work with foreign apps.
  - **How to get bolivianos:** withdraw in Uyuni on arrival (20 Jan, a Wednesday) and top up in Sucre and La Paz; bring ≈ US$150–200 in clean, untorn 2013+ $50/$100 notes as backup for a casa de cambio.
  - **Before the jeep tour:** carry **≈ Bs 250 in small notes** for park fees, the border tax and toilets (see stop 17). Buy them in San Pedro (casas de cambio on Caracoles) or carry USD/CLP the drivers will change at a worse rate.
- **Bolivia visa:** for a US passport, ✅ visa-free for up to 90 days since 1 Dec 2025 (US Embassy La Paz notice, 3 Dec 2025). Other passports: check.
- **Bolivia unrest (live risk):** on 24 Nov 2026 the government raised diesel to Bs 20.57/L; drivers blocked the Oruro–Potosí–Chuquisaca road the same day and Chuquisaca's drivers' federation called an **indefinite blockade from 25 Nov** (Correo del Sur, 24 Nov). A 53-day blockade wave ended in mid-November. Bus fares are likely to rise, and blockades can freeze the Uyuni → Potosí → Sucre buses for days. Re-check a week before 18 Jan (ABC Bolivia road map, Correo del Sur, Opinión); the Sucre → La Paz leg is already a flight.

**Weather on these dates (January station normals from Wikipedia's climate tables; La Paz from SENAMHI via the route brainstorm).** San Pedro 4 mm in January, but the *invierno altiplánico* hits the high ground: afternoon thunderstorms, hail or snow above ≈ 4,000 m. Uyuni 79 mm on 11 days; Potosí 105 mm / 18 days; Sucre 148 mm / 16 days; La Paz 134 mm / 21 days; Copacabana 185 mm / 19 days (the wettest stop of the trip). Mornings are usually the dry window everywhere, so every hike below starts early.

**Moon.** 37% on 14 Jan (sets ≈ midnight) → full on 22 Jan (Sucre) → 79% on 27 Jan. San Pedro's first two nights are the best for stars; the salt flat sunrise on 20 Jan has a bright moon (93%).

**Clocks.** Chile is on summer time in January (UTC−3, LP), the same as Argentina. Bolivia is UTC−4, so set your watch **back 1 h at Hito Cajón (18 Jan)**, and Peru is another hour behind (LP), so **back 1 h again at Kasani (29 Jan)**.

**Section tips from Lonely Planet** (*South America on a Shoestring*, researched ≈ 2019; per-stop details are in each guide, marked (LP)):
- **Safety:** LP says crime against tourists is rising in La Paz and, less so, in Copacabana. It names Potosí's mine tours, the Death Road bike trips and the Uyuni 4WD tours as the activities where cheap agencies cut safety corners, so choose those three on safety, not price. Stay away from protests and blockades (LP).
- **Scams:** fake police and "helpful" strangers are on the rise. Real police wear uniform and never make you get into a taxi with them, hand over your passport on demand or be searched in the street (LP).
- **Buses:** keep valuables in the day pack on your lap, watch your bag go into the hold, keep the baggage tag, and padlock the pack. Look at the bus before you buy; the tourist buses to Copacabana and Uyuni cost about double but are safer (LP).
- **Money:** refuse torn or very tatty notes (nobody will take them later) and only carry crisp US dollars. Bolivianos are hard to change outside Bolivia, so spend down before Peru (LP). In Chile, choose *tarjeta extranjera* at ATMs (fees up to ≈ US$10) and expect up to 6% extra on cards; San Pedro's ATMs often run dry (LP).
- **Cheap eating:** lunch (almuerzo) is the main meal, and the best value is the set lunch in and around the markets (LP).
- **Tipping (Bolivia):** 10–15% in restaurants; 10–20% for tour guides, whose pay is a small share of the tour price (LP).
- **Toilets:** ≈ B$1 in towns and ≈ B$5 out on the altiplano; toilet paper goes in the bin, and carry your own (LP).
- **Cheap rooms:** never run a propane heater in a closed room if one is offered (LP).
- **Borders:** small unofficial "administration fees" at remote posts are common; LP's advice is to pay and move on (LP).

## Open issues: Chile + Bolivia
0. **Chile's SAG food declaration:** The Broke Backpacker says the agricultural form is now filed online ≈ 48 h before entry. Check whether that applies at the Jama crossing on Fri 15 Jan.

1. **Bolivian transport strikes and blockades (biggest risk).** The diesel rise to Bs 20.57 (24 Nov 2026) triggered blockades on the Oruro–Potosí–Chuquisaca road and an indefinite Chuquisaca drivers' strike from 25 Nov. Re-check the week before 18 Jan. If roads are blocked: tour → Uyuni → fly Uyuni–La Paz (BoA/Amaszonas, not priced) and drop Potosí/Sucre, or wait it out in Sucre. Bus fares in this section (Tickets Bolivia, pre-rise) will likely go up.
2. **Hito Cajón closures** (storms, 1–3 days at a time in Jan): the jeep tour may wait or reroute via Ollagüe. The Potosí night is the buffer (go Uyuni → Sucre direct).
3. **Jeep tour 2027 prices aren't published yet** (WWT lists prices valid to 31 Dec 2026). Estrella del Sur's cancellation terms conflict on its own page; confirm before any deposit. Incahuasi will probably be skipped (flooding), so don't pay its fee upfront.
4. **Sucre → La Paz on Sun 24 Jan has no nonstop** on Google Flights: BoA via Cochabamba, 09:05 → 11:25, US$56 (live). EcoJet's new SRE–LPB route (from 4 Dec) shows no schedule; check flyecojet.aero before buying. BoA's nonstops are Tue/Wed/Thu only (unverified lead).
5. **Isla del Sol north end:** probably open (AllTrails hikes Aug–Oct 2026), but La Paz Life still calls it intermittently disputed. Ask the Copacabana hostel the day before; the fallback is the south half only.
6. **Unverified prices (≈):**
   - Pico Austria (1-day US$80–115, 2-day US$190–270): only platform listings and AI leads, no operator page read.
   - Uyuni/Potosí/Sucre counter bus fares, the Copacabana boats (Bs 30–40) and the island fees (Bs 25–55 in total).
   - Valle de Marte and Garganta del Diablo gates; bike hire; all food prices; the 22 Jan Bolivian holiday.
7. **Bed checks to repeat before booking:**
   - Hostal Lackuntur (the pick since 25 Nov): Booking only, so check the Genius price at checkout. Casa Voyage (backup 2): newest reviews, because of the March 2026 bed bugs.
   - Mia Posada and the Koala Den: Hostelworld only.
   - Los Faroles: only a family room was offered for 1 adult on Booking.
   - La Casa Bonita on Agoda (US$23.70): confirm the unit is the 4-bed dorm.
   - Google Maps showed no seller prices for any of the 11 properties tried, so the Maps index check added nothing here.
8. **Visa:** visa-free entry to Bolivia applies to US passports (since 1 Dec 2025); confirm for the passport actually used.
9. Nothing here needs a sign-in. Cerro Toco, Pico Austria and the Death Road are bookable on arrival; only the jeep tour and the San Pedro bed (Chilean high season) are worth reserving ≈ 1–2 weeks ahead.
10. **Where LP (≈ 2019) disagrees with the guides** (left as they are):
    - LP says US citizens need a US$160 Bolivian visa; the guides' 3 Dec 2025 US Embassy notice (visa-free) is newer and wins.
    - LP has Bolivian immigration in Uyuni town on the San Pedro tour; the operators' 2026 pages put it at Hito Cajón.
    - LP says Potosí's **Uyuni buses use the old terminal** (Av. Universitaria), not the Nueva Terminal the Potosí guide assumes. Check the ticket on 20 Jan.
    - LP lists the Tiquina launch as running 05:00–21:00; the Copacabana guide says the strait closes at 18:30. Either way the morning bus is fine.
    - Fees have moved a lot since LP: the Valle de la Luna (CLP 3,000 in LP vs ✅ CLP 10,800), the Potosí mint (B$40 + B$20 vs ✅ Bs 70) and the Casa de la Libertad (B$15 vs ✅ Bs 50).
