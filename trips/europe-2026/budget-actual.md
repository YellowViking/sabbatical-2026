# Europe 2026: actual spend vs the forecast

What the trip really cost, set against the [pre-trip budget forecast](budget-forecast.md). The figures come from card statements plus a log of cash spending, written day by day into a private expense sheet with the [card-expense-sync](../../skills/card-expense-sync/SKILL.md) tooling. Only totals by category, region and week are published here. Individual transactions stay private.

**Scope:** 23 Aug (the Seattle departure day) to 20 Nov 2026, so 90 days and 88 nights abroad. Everything is in US dollars.

## Bottom line

| | Total | Per day |
|---|---:|---:|
| All-in, with award redemptions counted at their value | **$12,160** | $135 |
| Award redemptions (SAS flight over, Hyatt Gothenburg, two Avios flights) | $831 | |
| **Cash spent** | **$11,329** | **$126** |

| **Difference** | **≈ $3.6–4.0k under (−25%)** | |

\*The forecast was **$15.6–16.3k for 83 nights**. For a like-for-like comparison, I removed insurance and the eSIM (not tracked here) and the 10% cushion. The living costs, sights and local costs are scaled to the 88 nights actually spent. Intercity transport is kept as planned, because it depends on the route rather than the number of nights. Against the original headline, the trip came in **about $4.3–5k under**, despite being a week longer than planned.

## Lessons for the next budget

- **Budget lodging at hostel prices, not private-room prices.** In practice, well-rated dorm beds and cheap private rooms averaged **$42/night against the $79 planned**. Treat private rooms as a deliberate extra, not the default.
- **An overland zigzag costs more than the planned backbone.** Spontaneous detours added roughly $300–500 to intercity transport: seven border-crossing train days, a ticket bought and not used, and a shared car. Budget transport per leg actually taken, and keep a separate line for route changes.
- **The food-and-drink model held up.** At about $46/day against $53, three drinks a day was realistic; the extra saving came from groceries and cheap local lunches.
- **Expensive regions stay expensive.** The Nordics came in within ±10% of the forecast. The forecast missed mostly in cheaper regions, where it was too cautious.
- **Award redemptions were worth about $830**, mostly the transatlantic flight over. Keep valuing them explicitly. A plan that counts them as free looks cheaper than the trip really was.

## Method

- **Sources:** Chase statements, pulled as JSON plus the statement CSV so that closed billing cycles are included. Amex charges and a few items paid on other cards were added by hand from confirmations. Cash spending was logged the same day.
- **Lodging** is spread over the nights it covers, not booked on the charge date. Lodging taxes and fees paid at the property are included.
- **A shared road trip and shared rooms** count only the traveller's half.
- **Points:** airline points are valued at 1.4¢ and Hyatt points at 1.7¢. Award taxes are counted as cash.
- **Excluded:** insurance, the eSIM, pre-trip gear, annual card fees, home subscriptions and tolls.
